# Power & Side-Channel Measurement

The PROACT board generates its own trimmable **0.8 V core rail** on-board and delivers it to the
chip through a **0.01 Ω sense shunt**, so the ChipWhisperer (or any scope) can capture the die's
instantaneous supply current as a power trace. This page covers the whole chain: LDO → trim →
route (`JP7`) → shunt (`R7`) → chip, and how to measure across it.

![Power path](../img/power_path.png)

> ⚠️ **Set Vcore to 0.800 V *before* the chip ever meets the board.** The full 6-step trim
> procedure is on [Getting-Started](Getting-Started.md) — do it first.

---

## Supply rails at a glance

- **`VDDIO` (3.3 V)** comes from the CW308 rail (`J8.14`) and supplies the PROACT I/O ring
  (chip pins 7 and 22), the USB bridge modules (via `S1-6/7`), the pull-ups and the `U2` LDO.
  For USB-only bench use off the ChipWhisperer, feed 3.3 V into the `VDDIO` test point instead.
- **`VDD core` (0.8 V)** is generated on-board by `U2`, trimmed with `R20`, routed by `JP7`,
  and reaches the chip's core pins **15** and **28** through the `R7` shunt.

```
 CW308 3.3 V (J8.14) ─────────────────────► VDDIO ─► PROACT pins 7, 22 · bridge modules · pull-ups
                                              │
                                              ▼
                     U2  TPS74801 LDO — trim R20/R21 → 0.80–0.90 V
                                              │
                          ┌─── JP7 1-2 ───────┤─── JP7 2-3 (direct) ──┐
                          ▼                                            │
              J8.8 FILTIN → CW308 L-C filter → J8.5/6 Vcore_back ──────┤
                                                                       ▼
                                    Vcore_shunt_1 (C5/C10 · J8.3 SHUNT-H)
                                                                       │
                                                            R7 0.01 Ω shunt
                                                                       │
                                      Vcore (die side · J8.2 SHUNT-L · no caps)
                                                                       ▼
                                                        PROACT core — pins 15, 28
```

---

## U2 TPS74801 — the trimmable core LDO

The 0.8 V core rail is generated by the **U2 TPS74801** LDO (1.5 A adjustable) and trimmed with
the multi-turn potentiometer **R20** (1 kΩ) against the feedback resistor **R21** (8.2 kΩ):

**V<sub>core</sub> = 0.8 V × (1 + R20 / 8.2 kΩ) → adjustable 0.80 – 0.90 V**

Turning `R20` fully down gives 0.800 V; the full 1 kΩ of trim raises the rail to about 0.90 V.
Trim it **with the chip out of the socket** — see the procedure on
[Getting-Started](Getting-Started.md).

---

## `JP7` — core-rail route

`JP7` chooses whether the 0.8 V rail goes to the shunt **directly** or takes a round trip through
the **CW308's L-C low-pass filter** first.

| Link | Route | When to use |
|:----:|-------|-------------|
| **1-2** | 0.8 V → `J8.8` (**FILTIN**) → **CW308 L-C low-pass filter** → back on `J8.5/6` → shunt | Side-channel capture on the CW308 — the filter cleans the rail so the shunt sees the die, not supply noise |
| **2-3** | 0.8 V → shunt, **direct** | Bench use off the CW308, or when you want the shortest supply path |

  <img src="../img/jumpers/JP7.png" alt="JP7 Vcore route" width="70%">
</p>

With `JP7` = 1-2, set the CW308 filter input to **victim-supplied (`FILTIN`)** — do **not** drive
it from `VADJ`. See [CW308 Setup](CW308-Setup.md) for the motherboard settings.

---

## `R7` — the 0.01 Ω current-sense shunt

After `JP7`, the rail crosses **`R7`**, a **0.01 Ω** shunt in the core supply path. Both sides of
the shunt are brought out on the CW308 East connector:

| `J8` pin | Net | Meaning |
|:--------:|-----|---------|
| 2  | `Vcore` (SHUNT-L) | Die side of the R7 sense shunt |
| 3  | `Vcore_shunt_1` (SHUNT-H) | Supply side of the shunt |
| 5, 6 | `Vcore_back` | Filtered 0.8 V **returning** from the CW308 L-C filter (used when `JP7` = 1-2) |
| 8  | 0.8 V (FILTIN) | Core rail from the U2 LDO **into** the CW308 filter (used when `JP7` = 1-2) |

The voltage across `R7` (SHUNT-H − SHUNT-L) is the power trace: every milliamp the die draws
appears as a voltage drop across the shunt.

### The die side is deliberately capacitor-free

Decoupling (`C5`/`C10`) sits *before* the shunt, on `Vcore_shunt_1`. The die side of the shunt —
net `Vcore`, chip pins **15** and **28** — carries **no capacitors at all**: only `R7`, the chip's
core pins, the `Vcore` test point and the `J8.2` SHUNT‑L sense tap live on that net. This is by design, for side-channel
fidelity: with nothing to buffer it, the instantaneous die current is forced through `R7` and
shows up, unfiltered, in the measured trace.

## Measurement chain

For a capture on the CW308:

1. **`JP7` → 1-2** — the 0.8 V rail leaves the board on `J8.8` (FILTIN), passes through the
   CW308's L-C low-pass filter, and returns on `J8.5/6` (`Vcore_back`). The filter removes supply
   noise upstream of the shunt.
2. The cleaned rail crosses **`R7`** and feeds the chip (pins 15/28).
3. The CW308 senses the drop across the shunt (SHUNT-H `J8.3` / SHUNT-L `J8.2`) and presents it
   on its **MEAS** SMA — connect MEAS to the ChipWhisperer capture input (or a scope).

**Sensitivity:** `R7` is **0.01 Ω**, so 1 mA of core current is 10 µV across the shunt — use the
ChipWhisperer low-noise amplifier on the `MEAS` output, or a differential probe directly in the
two `R7` probe holes.

For phase-locked (synchronous) sampling, the chip clock is always echoed to the scope on `J7.5`
(no jumper needed) — see [Clock System](Clock-System.md).

---

## Probing guidance

| Where | How |
|-------|-----|
| **`R7` probe holes** | Two probe holes flank the shunt — designed for a **differential probe** directly across `R7`, as an alternative to the CW308 `MEAS` path |
| **`Vcore` test point** | Die-side, capacitor-free node — probe **high-impedance only**; it is also the trim target for the 0.8 V procedure |
| **`VDDIO` test point** | Probe or feed the 3.3 V I/O rail |
| **`GND` test point** | Scope / meter reference |
